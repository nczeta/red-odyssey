01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

Making Martian terrain
understandable.

Exploring a planet means understanding not only its landmarks, but also the terrain that connects them.

Red Odyssey is an interactive planetary exploration platform focused on the Martian surface. By combining orbital imagery, topographic data and geospatial analysis, it provides an integrated environment for investigating the landscape and exploring possible routes across the terrain.

By bringing different sources of planetary data into one environment, Red Odyssey connects surface observation with terrain analysis and route planning. Users can explore geographical features, compare spatial information and investigate how topography influences possible exploration paths.

02 / HOW IT WORKS

From observation
to exploration.

Red Odyssey brings together planetary observations, geospatial processing and interactive mapping to transform Martian data into an environment for scientific exploration.

  1. 01

    Planetary observations

    Collecting and integrating orbital imagery and elevation datasets to build a detailed representation of the Martian surface.

  2. 02

    GIS & terrain processing

    Processing geospatial data, aligning coordinate systems and deriving terrain information through QGIS and Python.

  3. 03

    Geospatial layers

    Organizing imagery, elevation models and derived terrain products into interoperable map layers.

  4. 04

    Interactive exploration

    Exploring the Martian landscape through OpenLayers, with interactive navigation, layer management and spatial inspection.

  5. 05

    Intelligent route planning

    Developing a terrain-aware routing workflow to evaluate terrain costs and identify possible exploration routes.

04 / TERRAIN ANALYSIS

Understanding the shape
of Martian terrain.

The terrain analysis is based on the CTX Digital Elevation Model of Jezero Crater, with a spatial resolution of 20 metres per pixel. The DEM is used to derive complementary views of the surface in QGIS: hillshade, elevation contours and slope.

Elevation & relief
The Digital Elevation Model represents surface elevation in metres and provides the topographic basis for the other derived products.
Slope
Calculated from the DEM in QGIS. The explorer displays the supplied color visualization, with a qualitative legend from least steep to steepest terrain; numeric slope values are not available.
Contour lines
Generated from the same DEM in QGIS at 200-metre intervals. Each contour connects locations with the same elevation.
Hillshade
Derived from the DEM in QGIS using multidirectional simulated illumination. It helps reveal surface morphology, but does not represent real lighting conditions or a photograph.
Terrain interpretation is not a safety certification.

These products describe selected topographic properties of the study area. They do not, on their own, establish rover traversability, route safety or operational suitability.

05 / ROUTE PLANNING

Between two points,
the terrain matters.

Route planning aims to connect a starting point and a destination while accounting for the characteristics of the Martian terrain. By combining elevation data and terrain-derived information, the system is designed to support the evaluation of possible paths across Jezero Crater.

A

Define the question

Define a starting point and a destination within the mapped area. These two endpoints establish the spatial framework for the route-planning process.

↳

Evaluate the terrain

Terrain information will be used to assess the characteristics of the landscape between the selected endpoints. This step is intended to provide the foundation for evaluating route feasibility.

B

Interpret the result

The planned output includes a geographic route representation and relevant metrics, such as distance and elevation changes. These results will make it possible to interpret and compare potential paths.

07 / DATA SOURCES & CREDITS

Every layer needs
a traceable source.

Red Odyssey uses CTX orbital imagery and elevation data of Jezero Crater provided by USGS Astrogeology. Additional terrain products, including hillshade, contour lines and slope maps, are derived from the original elevation model through processing in QGIS.

The explorer distributes rendered 20 m/pixel maps and contour vectors. The imagery source resolution is 5 m/pixel; elevation and slope colors are visualizations, rather than numeric measurements sampled by the browser.
